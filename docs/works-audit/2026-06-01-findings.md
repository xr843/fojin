## Manual precision review (sample of 40 Pass-1 multi-witness works)

The sample is the 20 largest Pass-1 clusters + 20 random Pass-1 multi-witness works.

**False-MERGE rate: low (~3–5%).** The within-Taishō "same Indic work, multiple Chinese translations" groupings are overwhelmingly correct — e.g. 心经 (`p-p-hrdaya`, 5), 金刚经 (`vajracchedika`, 4), 法华 (`saddharmapundarika`, 4), and many dharaṇī with genuinely multiple Chinese translations (`anantamukha-sadhaka-dharani` 9, `mahamayuri-vidyarajni` 6, `usnisavijaya` 5). These are real.

- **One likely false merge:** `mahaparinirvanasutra` (display 佛般泥洹經, 5 witnesses). "Mahāparinirvāṇa" is a homonym across two distinct works — the early/mainstream 般泥洹經 and the Mahāyāna 涅槃經 (tathāgatagarbha). Normalizing both to one key risks merging them. Needs a spot-check of the 5 members.

**The dominant defect is RECALL (false splits), not precision.** The macro stat (only 14 cross-canon works) is confirmed at the row level:
- 金刚经 exists as **two** Works: `sk-vajracchedika` (id 369, Chinese, 4 witnesses) and `sk-vajracchedika-prajnaparamita` (id 841, gretil/Sanskrit, 2). They should be one. The normalized Skt keys differ only by a `-prajnaparamita` suffix.
- 迦叶品 exists as **two** Works: `sk-kasyapaparivarta` (id 416, Chinese, 3) and `sk-kasyapaparivartasutra` (id 818, gretil, 2). Split by a `-sutra` suffix.
- `dharmapada-udanavarga` (法句經, 2) conflates two related-but-distinct collections in one key — the opposite failure (over-broad key).

Root cause: cross-canon linkage depends entirely on `title_sa` (Sanskrit title) matching after a literal normalization. Most texts have no `title_sa` bridge (all 9,293 pass2/3/4 works), and even when both a Chinese and a Sanskrit witness exist, trivial key-string differences (`-sutra`, `-prajnaparamita`) prevent the merge.

## Decision gate — outcome

Per the plan's gate table:

> `multi_witness_ratio` very low (<15%) **and** `cross_canon_works` small → the spine is mostly singletons — a catalog, not an aggregation. The 跨藏 payoff barely exists yet. **Do NOT build Phase 1/2 on a hollow spine.**

This case is unambiguous: **1.4% multi-witness, 14 cross-canon works.** The spine, as currently built, does not yet aggregate.

**Routing:**
- **Do NOT build Phase 1 (governance tooling) or Phase 2 (Work-level aligned reading) yet.** There are only 14 cross-canon works to align and ~141 groupings to govern — both far too small to justify the investment, and the precision is already acceptable.
- **The real next phase is RECALL improvement in `build_works`** — turning the 98.6% singletons into genuine cross-canon aggregations. Concretely (each becomes its own measured plan):
  1. **Loosen Skt-title key normalization** to fold trivial suffix/variant differences (`-sutra`, `-prajnaparamita`, transliteration variants) — recovers obvious false splits like the Diamond Sutra and Kāśyapaparivarta. Cheapest, highest-yield.
  2. **Add non-Skt-title bridges.** The biggest lever: link witnesses using data fojin already has —
     - `alignment_pairs` (texts already aligned ⇒ same work) — a direct, high-confidence bridge currently unused by build_works;
     - SuttaCentral parallels (SC ↔ Āgama/Taishō) to link the 4,948 SC singletons;
     - Toh ↔ Taishō concordances to link the 779 Toh singletons.
  3. **Guard the homonym false-merge** (early vs Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa) — secondary; merge-error rate is low.
- **Re-run this audit after each recall change** and watch `cross_canon_works` / `multi_witness_ratio` move. Governance (Phase 1) and aligned reading (Phase 2) only become worth building once those numbers are materially non-trivial.

## What this audit changes

The pre-audit roadmap assumed the spine was built and the next step was to govern/align it. The data shows the spine is **structurally hollow** — it relabels texts 1:1 rather than aggregating them. The single highest-leverage FRBR investment is **recall**, not governance, not aligned reading, and definitely not adding more data sources.

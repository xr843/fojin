## Manual precision review (sample of 40 Pass-1 multi-witness works)

The sample is the 20 largest Pass-1 clusters + 20 random Pass-1 multi-witness works.

**False-MERGE rate: low (~3–5%).** The within-Taishō "same Indic work, multiple Chinese translations" groupings are overwhelmingly correct — e.g. 心经 (`p-p-hrdaya`, 5), 金刚经 (`vajracchedika`, 4), 法华 (`saddharmapundarika`, 4), and many dharaṇī with genuinely multiple Chinese translations (`anantamukha-sadhaka-dharani` 9, `mahamayuri-vidyarajni` 6, `usnisavijaya` 5). These are real.

- **One likely false merge:** `mahaparinirvanasutra` (display 佛般泥洹經, 5 witnesses). "Mahāparinirvāṇa" is a homonym across two distinct works — the early/mainstream 般泥洹經 and the Mahāyāna 涅槃經 (tathāgatagarbha). Normalizing both to one key risks merging them. Needs a spot-check of the 5 members.

**The dominant defect is RECALL (false splits), not precision.** The macro stat (only 14 cross-canon works) is confirmed at the row level:
- 金刚经 exists as **two** Works: `sk-vajracchedika` (id 369, Chinese, 4 witnesses) and `sk-vajracchedika-prajnaparamita` (id 841, gretil/Sanskrit, 2). They should be one. The normalized Skt keys differ only by a `-prajnaparamita` suffix.
- 迦叶品 exists as **two** Works: `sk-kasyapaparivarta` (id 416, Chinese, 3) and `sk-kasyapaparivartasutra` (id 818, gretil, 2). Split by a `-sutra` suffix.
- `dharmapada-udanavarga` (法句經, 2) conflates two related-but-distinct collections in one key — the opposite failure (over-broad key).

Root cause: within-spine cross-canon linkage depends entirely on `title_sa` (Sanskrit title) matching after a literal normalization. Even when both a Chinese and a Sanskrit witness exist, trivial key-string differences (`-sutra`, `-prajnaparamita`) prevent the merge.

### Architectural caveat — most singletons are BY DESIGN (do not read this as pure recall failure)

fojin already has a **deliberate two-layer** design (verified in code):
- **Work spine** = "the same work, multiple translations" (one Indic original's translation lineage). Pass-1 Skt-title clustering is its only aggregation mechanism.
- **`text_relations`** (table `text_relations`, served by `GET /api/alignment/canonical/{text_id}`, source `suttacentral`) = **sutta-level Pali↔Chinese academic parallels** (Akanuma-style). This is a separate, complementary layer — cross-canon Pali↔Chinese correspondence is intentionally handled here, NOT in the Work spine.

So the 4,948 SC + 779 Toh singletons are **not all "recall failures."** Their cross-canon correspondences (where they exist) live in `text_relations` by design. Counting them naively as missed Work-merges would conflict with the existing architecture. Likewise, `alignment_pairs` is pairwise/collection-level and is **not** a valid work-identity signal — `build_works` correctly does not use it.

The genuinely-fixable **Work-spine** recall defect is therefore narrower than the raw singleton ratio suggests: it is the **Skt-key false-splits** (Sanskrit and Chinese witnesses of the *same work* landing in two Works because their normalized keys differ).

## Decision gate — outcome

Per the plan's gate table:

> `multi_witness_ratio` very low (<15%) **and** `cross_canon_works` small → the spine is mostly singletons — a catalog, not an aggregation. The 跨藏 payoff barely exists yet. **Do NOT build Phase 1/2 on a hollow spine.**

This case is unambiguous: **1.4% multi-witness, 14 cross-canon works.** The spine, as currently built, does not yet aggregate.

**Routing:**
- **Do NOT build Phase 1 (governance tooling) or Phase 2 (Work-level aligned reading) yet.** There are only 14 cross-canon works to align and ~141 groupings to govern — both far too small to justify the investment, and the precision is already acceptable.
- **The real next phase is narrow, high-confidence Work-spine recall — NOT a broad bridging effort:**
  1. **Loosen Skt-title key normalization** to fold trivial suffix/variant differences (`-sutra`, `-prajnaparamita`, transliteration variants) — recovers genuine false splits like the Diamond Sutra (`vajracchedika` Chinese vs `vajracchedika-prajnaparamita` Sanskrit) and Kāśyapaparivarta. **Cheapest, highest-yield, and unambiguously correct** (same Indic work, currently in two Works). This alone raises `cross_canon_works` by reuniting Skt witnesses with their Chinese clusters.
  2. **Guard over-broad keys** (e.g. `dharmapada-udanavarga` conflating two collections) and the homonym false-merge (early vs Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa). Secondary; the merge-error rate is low.
  3. **Open design question for the maintainer (NOT a unilateral recommendation):** should the Work spine *expand its scope* to aggregate same-work translations across canons via signals other than Skt-title (e.g. Toh↔Taishō translation concordances, where a Tibetan and a Chinese translation share one Indic original)? This is a scholarly call about the boundary between "same work" (Work spine) and "related parallel" (`text_relations`). It deliberately does **not** include folding `text_relations` Pali↔Chinese parallels into Works — those are complementary by design.
- **Re-run this audit after each change** and watch `cross_canon_works` / `multi_witness_ratio`. Governance (Phase 1) and aligned reading (Phase 2) only become worth building once those numbers are materially non-trivial — but note that even at scale, the cross-canon reading payoff may legitimately span **both** the Work spine and `text_relations`, not the Work spine alone.

## What this audit changes

The pre-audit roadmap assumed the spine was built and the next step was to govern/align it. The data shows within-Work aggregation is currently tiny (141 multi-witness, 14 cross-canon). **But the headline 98.6%-singleton figure must NOT be read as "the spine is broken"** — much of it is by-design, because cross-canon Pali↔Chinese correspondence lives in the complementary `text_relations` layer, not the Work spine.

Corrected conclusion: (1) **don't build heavy governance or aligned-reading on the Work spine yet** — there are too few within-Work cross-canon works to justify it; (2) the one clearly-worth-doing Work-spine fix is **Skt-key false-split recovery** (cheap, correct, e.g. reuniting the Diamond Sutra's Sanskrit and Chinese witnesses); (3) whether to *expand* the Work spine's scope beyond Skt-title matching is a scholarly design question for the maintainer, deliberately kept distinct from the `text_relations` parallel layer. Adding more data sources remains the wrong move regardless.
